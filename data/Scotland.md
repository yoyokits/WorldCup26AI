# Scotland 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴 — Historical Tournament Analysis & Trends

## Historical Performance Arc
Scotland's WC history is both rich and heartbreaking. Nine appearances (1954–1998) but never once advanced beyond the group stage — often eliminated on goal difference. Famous near-misses: 1974 unbeaten but eliminated; 1978 (Archie Gemmill's wonder goal vs Holland); 1982 group exit despite beating New Zealand. Absent since 1998 until this qualification. 28 years in the wilderness makes 2026 a massive moment.

## Statistical Trends
- **Goals Scored/Conceded:** 1.4 avg attack / 1.3 defense in recent qualifying run. More attacking than past Scotland sides.
- **Set-Piece Efficiency:** McTominay and Robertson provide elite set-piece delivery. Scotland average 2.3 corners per goal in qualifying.
- **Style:** High-energy press, vertical transition football under Steve Clarke. Not the cautious side of old.

## Key Players & Tactical Notes
- **Scott McTominay (Napoli, CM):** The tournament talisman. His bicycle kick against Denmark in qualifying is already iconic. Napoli move elevated him to world-class level — scores big goals and controls midfield.
- **Andrew Robertson (Liverpool, LB):** Still elite at 32. One of the world's best overlapping left-backs. Champions League experience essential.
- **Kieran Tierney (Real Sociedad, LB):** Can play CB or LB — tactical flexibility. Injury concern pre-tournament.
- **Ryan Christie (Bournemouth, AM):** Work-rate and creativity off the bench.
- **Billy Gilmour (Brighton, CM):** Injury concern — his absence would be a significant blow to midfield quality.
- **Tactic:** 4-3-3 high press, Robertson + McTominay as key outlets.

## Matchup History & Tendencies
- **vs Brazil:** Scotland's last WC game in 1998 was the opening match vs Brazil — they drew 1-1 before losing narrowly later. Historical precedent for competing.
- **vs Morocco:** Both teams organized and physical — a 1-1 draw or tight contest is probable.
- **vs Haiti:** Scotland should win this but must not underestimate Haiti's pace.

## Analyst Verdict for 2026
Scotland's best qualifying campaign in decades under Steve Clarke. McTominay's elevation to world-class changes Scotland's ceiling — previously they had no player who could single-handedly win a WC game; now they do. If Gilmour is fit, the midfield is competitive at international level. Target: advance from group for first time in history — Morocco is the key result.
